Dataset 1 rejected: 1,145,965 rows but only 269 unique descriptions and 106 unique skill lists (each description reused about 4,000 times), so the data is synthetic and templated. Checked with nunique() before building on it.
Dataset 2: 2,277 job ads, 15 tech job titles, no missing values.                         Issues: Indian ads (₹ salaries), about 2020–21 (COVID references), lots of boilerplate text, no data or AI titles.                                                                         Decision: use it to build the pipeline, then switch to a recent, broader dataset.
Investigating ads with no skills found: The first run found no skills in 154 of 2,275 ads (6.8%), and 65% of those were database or network administrator ads. I found two causes.     1. The skills list was missing sysadmin, DBA and AI terms, so I added Windows Server, Active Directory, LAN/WAN, PL/SQL, T-SQL, “ai/ml” and others. After re-running, ads with no skills dropped to 116 (5.1%). Database administrator ads fell from 59 to 44 and network administrator ads from 41 to 24. Adding “ai” also showed that 40.1% of machine learning ads mention Artificial Intelligence.                                                              2. Most of the remaining ads are not tech jobs at all: executive assistant, data entry, a maths tutoring school, and generic company or IT-executive descriptions. The dataset’s creator seems to have collected them under “administrator” titles by mistake.          Lesson: looking into the failures exposed a data quality problem that the overall numbers alone wouldn’t have shown.
